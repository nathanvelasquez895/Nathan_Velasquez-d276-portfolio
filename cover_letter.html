My Journey through adversity to resilience

My path to IT and Computer Science hasn't been the standard ideal, but these struggles have shaped how I now approach difficult tasks. If I could get through worse situations by problem-solving, I can excel through anything.

Coming from a family predisposed to mental health struggles and not having enough income for treatment, my starting line felt miles behind everyone else. I endured a childhood and early adult life of struggling with financial and mental health issues. But I never gave up and came out on the other side completely financially stable, with the treatment I needed - spending 10 years straight saving up enough to go to college full-time comfortably.

High-Pressure Accountability & Empathetic Support

Before finally being able to dedicate myself to a full-time Computer Science degree, I was the manager of a group home for autistic adults. This role was the epitome of high-stakes conflict resolution and unpredictability, often involving weekly middle-of-the-night crisis management. Be it client outbursts or no-call, no-shows from my employees.

In this environment, a "normal" day hardly existed. Adaptability was the core requirement for nearly every situation. Additionally, I stepped up to become the de facto IT specialist for the home. Fixing the home's printer, setting up health-monitoring devices for clients, and training non-native English-speaking staff on our complex data-tracking system. For these employees, data tracking could sometimes take 2 straight hours, since they are caregivers, not professional writers. I took it upon myself to create AI templates for these employees. With an attention to omitting OSHA-violating details, yet turning their general statements into fully coherent and compliant data.

Adapting, Engineering, and Accelerating

Going into a math-heavy degree like Computer Science, I was at a severe disadvantage because I had not taken a math course in about 16 years, since high school. I had completely forgotten nearly all basic algebra concepts during this time. Instead of letting this be a roadblock, I engineered my own solution. Simultaneously designing, QA testing, auditing, and actually using my own custom learning platform, utilizing my custom AI math prompt template to provide incremental steps to understanding advanced math concepts. The results speak for themselves, as this hard work allowed me to accelerate through Statistics, Calculus, and both Discrete Mathematics 1 & 2 within my very first term. Crossing the purely math classes off the list for the degree.

This experience taught me something very important. Even if I'm in a situation where an immense amount of prerequisite knowledge is expected, and I am not equipped with it, I can adapt. The DM2 course required about 490 concepts to learn; by the end, I had created thousands of practice questions that scaffolded toward full knowledge. This definitely required more work than expected; however, it resulted in my passing the class ahead of schedule.

What Drives Me

Whether I'm acting as a crisis worker in residential care, maintaining compliance enforcement, accurate documentation, and front-line communication in my current security job, or analyzing fact accuracy and user experience in my current Data Annotation job, my standards are always the same. I consistently show up, take full ownership of my tasks, and am never satisfied until I resolve all issues to the absolute best of my abilities, ensuring every person I interact with has a resolution to their problem.
