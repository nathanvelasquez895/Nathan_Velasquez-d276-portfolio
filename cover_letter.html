My Journey:
Accountability, Grit, and Problem Solving

My path to IT and Computer Science hasn't been standard, but it has shaped the work ethic and diagnostic mindset I bring to technology every day.

Coming from a background where every resource was fought for, I learned early on the weight of financial responsibility and resilience. After overcoming significant personal challenges, I spent ten years straight saving every dollar I could to self-fund my higher education, refusing to let fear or circumstance hold me back from my goals.

High-Pressure Accountability & Empathetic Support

Before transitioning full-time into Computer Science, I served as the Manager of a residential group home for autistic adults. It was a role defined by high stakes, constant conflict resolution, and unpredictability, frequently requiring me to respond to middle-of-the-night emergencies or step in during intense crises.

In that environment, adaptability wasn't an abstract concept; it was a daily requirement. I became the de facto IT specialist for the facility. Troubleshooting office hardware, setting up assistive technologies for residents, and training non-native English speaking staff on complex data-tracking systems. To make technical reporting accessible to everyone, I engineered custom AI-driven templates to help my team verify their documentation accuracy.

Adapting, Engineering, and Accelerating

When I finally launched into my B.S. in Computer Science at WGU, I hadn't taken a math course in 16 years. Instead of letting that gap slow me down, I built my own study routines and engineering tools to bridge it. Through sheer focus, I accelerated through Applied Probability & Statistics, Calculus, and Discrete Mathematics in my very first term.

When standard course textbooks fell short and weren't working for my learning style, I didn't give up. I adapted. I engineered my own interactive, local learning platform featuring 491 practice topics to master Discrete Math concepts, combining custom prompt engineering, frontend code, and structured logic to teach myself the material effectively. Recognizing that others were likely facing the same hurdles, I published the project on GitHub and shared it with fellow students to help them succeed in the course as well.

What Drives Me

Whether managing emergency protocols in residential care, maintaining total vigilance during a security guard night shift, or analyzing code accuracy as an AI Data Annotator, my core standard remains unchanged: I show up, I take full ownership, and I am not satisfied until the issue is solved and the person behind the problem is taken care of.
